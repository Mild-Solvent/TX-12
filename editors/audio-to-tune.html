    Help

    Workflow
    1. Open an audio file (anything your browser plays: mp3, wav, ogg, m4a, flac…) or a MIDI file. Nothing is uploaded; it all runs in this page.
    2. Select a short part on the waveform (the chorus, 20-60 s). The radio has little memory: a song should stay under about 400 tokens.
    3. Audio: the page tracks the pitch every 10 ms (YIN), cuts it into notes where the pitch changes or a new attack is heard, and drops quiet or noisy parts as rests.
      MIDI: pick the track/channel that carries the tune, or "highest note" to take the top note across all tracks.
    4. Tempo: detected automatically (or read from the MIDI). If the tune plays at double or half speed press ×2 / ÷2 or type the BPM. The grid ("units per beat") sets the shortest step; u = 60 / (BPM × units per beat).
    5. Press Beeper to hear it as a square wave like the radio, Original for the same stretch of the source, A/B or Both to compare.
    6. Export a Rhythm song file (the chart is made for you) or the u + tune for a Geometry Dash level.
    Limitations (read this)
    • The radio plays one note at a time. A full song has many at once (singer, chords, bass, drums). The detector follows the strongest periodic sound, which is not always the melody. Expect wrong notes and gaps on band recordings; fix them in the Rhythm editor.
    • Results are best on: MIDI files, whistling/humming, a solo instrument, a simple synth lead, chiptunes.
    • "Centre channel only" helps when the vocal is mixed to the middle and the instruments are spread left/right. It does nothing for mono files.
    • Octave mistakes happen (a note jumps an octave). Fast runs, slides and vibrato become several short notes; try a larger "min note" or "merge same-pitch neighbours".
    • Tempo detection can land on half or double the real tempo. That still plays correctly; it only changes the grid.
    • MIDI tempo changes are ignored: the whole piece is quantized at its first tempo (so it stays on the grid).
    • The beeper is good from about 150 Hz to 4 kHz (D#3-B7). "Auto octave" and "fold" keep notes there.
    The Rhythm chart
    • Every note is played. A note becomes a circle unless it comes too soon after the previous circle (the chart density slider: from 0.6 s apart down to 0.18 s); those are marked x (music only).
    • Notes at least "hold" seconds long become holds (h); with sliders on, every other long note becomes a slider (s).
    • Tied continuations of long notes are always music only.
    • Density 1-3 is labelled EASY, 4-6 NORMAL, 7-8 HARD, 9-10 EXPERT, with the approach time a of the stock songs. You can type your own.
    Token format
    • Token = note (C C# D D# E F F# G G# A A# B) + octave digit + length digit (1-9 units of u seconds) + optional mark (Rhythm only). R1-R9 = rest.
    • Rhythm file: -- @Name|Difficulty then return { u = …, a = …, m = "…" }.
    • Geometry Dash: level fields u and m, same tokens without marks, looped.
